A primer for business owners

Your business is about to get a new kind of customer. Most owners aren’t ready.

In the next few years, more of your customers will find you through an AI assistant than through a Google search. This is a 7-minute course on what that means, why it matters, and what you can do about it, in plain English, no jargon.

CHAPTER 01

It’s 1998 again.

In 1998, most business owners thought they didn’t need a website. The phone book was still working. Customers still walked in. The internet was “for tech companies.”

By 2005, every business needed one. The owners who built one early got found. The ones who waited spent the next decade catching up.

We’re in the exact same window right now, but with AI. And the gap from “optional” to “mandatory” is closing a lot faster this time.

This course is short on purpose. By the end, you’ll know what’s actually changing, why it matters for your business specifically, and what you can do without becoming a software engineer.

CHAPTER 04

AI assistants aren’t just answering questions. They’re doing things.

This is the part most people miss. AI assistants have crossed a line in the last year. They used to summarize. Now they act on your behalf.

Watch what your customers are already doing:

  • “Find me a vegetarian-friendly restaurant near downtown that’s open right now and book a table for two at 7.”
  • “Which HVAC company near me has the best reviews and can come tomorrow morning? Get me a quote.”
  • “Order my regular from the closest taco place. Make it gluten-free this time.”

The customer isn’t browsing your website. They aren’t scrolling through Yelp. They’re telling an AI what they want, and the AI is picking, comparing, and booking on their behalf.

The new aisle

Think of it like a new shopping aisle that your competitors are already on. You can’t see it. You can’t walk down it. But your customers are already there, and the AI is picking what’s on the shelf. Right now, if your business isn’t formatted to be on that shelf, it doesn’t exist to those customers.

CHAPTER 05

GEO: the new SEO.

You probably know SEO (Search Engine Optimization). It’s the work of making Google show your business when someone searches for what you do. Every business owner has paid someone for SEO at some point, even if they didn’t love it.

GEO is the same idea, but for AI.

GEO  Generative Engine Optimization. Making sure AI assistants can find, understand, trust, and recommend your business when their users ask.

Here’s the key difference, and why GEO is a bigger deal than SEO ever was:

SEO was about ranking. GEO is about being the answer.

On Google, customers see ten blue links. They can click yours even if you’re #4. With AI, the customer gets one answer. Either the AI recommends you, or it doesn’t. There is no “page two.”

The businesses that figure out GEO early are going to dominate their category for the same reason early-SEO businesses dominated 2005-2015. The window is open right now.

CHAPTER 06

What’s an MCP endpoint? (One paragraph.)

MCP  Model Context Protocol. It’s the new standard that AI assistants use to read information from a business, and to take actions like checking your hours, comparing your prices, or booking an appointment, without bothering you every time.

Plain English: it’s a tidy, structured way for AI assistants to know what your business does, what you’re offering, and what you’re willing to let them do for customers on your behalf.

You stay in control.

You decide what AI assistants can see (hours, services, deals), what they can do (take a booking, send a quote), and what they always need your approval for (anything over $X, anything outside your normal services). They stop having to guess. You stop missing customers.

Think of an MCP endpoint as your business’s passport for the AI internet. Without one, AI assistants make do with whatever scraps they can find online. With one, your business shows up cleanly, currently, and ready to take action.

The cost of doing nothing.

It’s easy to read all this and think, “Sure, but I’ll deal with it when it’s mainstream.” That’s exactly what 1998’s holdouts told themselves about websites. Here’s what waiting actually costs you, in concrete terms:

  • →
    Every time a customer asks an AI assistant for a recommendation in your category and it picks a competitor instead of you, because that competitor is readable and you’re not. That’s a customer you’ll never know you lost.
  • →
    Every time the AI quotes wrong hours from a stale Yelp listing and your customer shows up to a closed door, they don’t blame the AI. They blame you.
  • →
    Every time someone says “book me a slot with your competitor” instead of “book me a slot with you,” you’re paying for that visibility gap in revenue you’ll never count.
  • →
    Every month you wait, more of your competitors will get this right, and you’ll be the one paying double to catch up later, exactly like late SEO adopters did.
The customers aren’t coming back to Google to double-check. The AI’s answer is the answer.

5. Have you defined what AI assistants are allowed to commit to on your behalf?
