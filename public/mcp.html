CHAPTER 01

The three letters nobody explains.

Every article about getting your business ready for AI eventually drops the same three letters: MCP. Then it moves on, as if you already knew. Most owners nod along and quietly hope it doesn’t matter. It does, and it’s simpler than it sounds.

An MCP is how an AI assistant safely reads your business and does things for your customers, without guessing, and without ever stepping outside the rules you set.

To see why it matters, you have to start with an uncomfortable fact: the website you already paid for can’t do this job. Not because it’s a bad website, but because it was built for people, not for machines. That’s the next chapter, and it’s the whole reason MCP exists.

CHAPTER 02

Why an AI can’t actually use your website.

Your website looks clean to you because your eyes do an enormous amount of work without you noticing. You skip the cookie banner, ignore the menu, scroll past the hero image, and land on the one line you wanted. An AI assistant can’t do that. It receives the whole page as code, and has to fight through all of it.

Behind that tidy page is a pile of markup, scripts, tracking tags, pop-ups, navigation, and styling, none of which means anything to a customer’s question. The AI has to wade through that noise and guess which scrap is your real answer. Type a business name to see what it’s actually up against:

What it reads from your MCP

But the deeper problem isn’t reading, it’s acting. Even if the AI guesses your hours right, there is no safe way for it to do anything on a web page it’s only half-understanding. It can’t be trusted to fill in and submit your booking form, or send a quote request, because it doesn’t know your rules and nobody ever told it what it’s allowed to do. So it does the safe thing: nothing. It gives a vague answer and tells the customer to call you, and the customer moves on.

A website is a poster. An AI needs a service counter.

A poster is for a person to stand back and read. A service counter is where a request gets handled, by someone who knows the rules and is allowed to act. Your website is the poster. An MCP is the counter, and almost no business has one yet.

CHAPTER 03

So what is an MCP?

MCP  Model Context Protocol. The word protocol just means “an agreed-upon standard,” like a wall outlet that any device can plug into. MCP is the standard shape that lets any AI assistant, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and the rest, plug into your business and work with it directly.

For your purposes, an MCP gives an AI two things your website never could:

  • A clean, structured version of your business it can read directly, your services, prices, hours, and current deals as plain labeled facts, with none of the page code to fight through.
  • A controlled counter where it can take the actions you’ve approved, booking, quoting, taking an order, answering a question, and nothing else.

What an MCP is not.

It is not a chatbot bolted onto your site, not a directory listing, and not “your website with extra steps.” It’s a separate, machine-readable doorway with rules built in. Your website stays exactly as it is, for the humans; the MCP works alongside it, for the AI.

And you don’t build it. A service like Bodji Beacon reads your existing website once, pulls out the real facts, checks them, and compiles them into that clean doorway, then keeps it current and under your control. Your job is to decide the rules, which is the next two chapters.

CHAPTER 04

Two doors: one to read, one to act.

This is the part that makes an MCP safe. It isn’t one open door, it’s two, and they’re very different. Click each one.

The read door is open to the world, because reading your hours can’t hurt anyone. The action door is locked by default, and only opens for an AI you’ve specifically authorized, to do the specific things you’ve allowed. That separation is the whole reason an AI can be useful to your customers without being a risk to your business.
